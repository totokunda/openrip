# CapCut's draft covers as a parity check — 2026-10-16

Every CapCut draft folder holds `draft_cover.jpg`: CapCut's own render of the draft's first frame, at the canvas size, with animations applied. `openrip-dev cover-parity <projects-root>` renders the same frame with OpenRip and compares the two, across every local draft whose first-frame media is still on disk. It needs no export from CapCut, so it can be re-run after every change. Drafts are numbered, not named.

18 drafts compare (36 have media missing at the first frame).

JPEG limits a perfect match to about 35–42 dB, which is where the simple drafts land (photo or SDR video, text). What still differs, by cause:

| 3 | 23–28 dB | about 1 px offsets in varying directions on video with motion, plus JPEG softening (covers carry 5–24% less fine detail than our renders); CapCut shows the same source frame we do | none found yet |

Three findings came out of this check:
- **Line wrapping.** It was missing. The covers measured the rule; see [text-wrap.md](../text-wrap.md).
- **HLG phone video.** CapCut tone-maps it to SDR; see [hdr.md](../hdr.md).
- **Synthetic bold and italic.** CapCut's B and I buttons were ignored. Bold is set on about 365 text runs in the local drafts and italic on 23. Italic is a 10° shear (`italic_degree`), which matches the cover. Bold widens the glyphs by `bold_width` (0.008) in the stroke's unit; calibration session 5 measures its strength.
