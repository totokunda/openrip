# Video masks against draft covers (2026-10-16)

CapCut 9.5.0, macOS. Two local drafts use a mask, both a plain rectangle (no rotation, feather or rounded corners) on a clip that starts at 0 s, so `draft_cover.jpg` is CapCut's render of it. Their media had been moved to an external disk; the comparison used copies of the drafts with the paths changed.

`openrip-dev cover-parity` on the two drafts:

| Draft | Clip | Mask | Before (no masks) | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 9:16 photo on a 9:16 canvas | 0.5 × 0.28, centre 0.127 up | whole photo shown | 30.8 dB, 1.0% of pixels off by > 32 |
| B | 16:9 video on a 16:9 canvas | 0.28 × 0.5, centred | whole frame shown | 33.8 dB, 0.6% off by > 32 |

- Mask edges: in B the box covers the same columns (259–460) and rows (101–277) as CapCut's. In A CapCut's box spans columns 101–303 and rows 214–414, exactly the pixel-centre step the bundle's maths gives; ours sits half a pixel right and one row up, and so does the photo inside it (aligning the two pictures takes the same shift), so the offset is in the photo's placement on that odd-width (1081) canvas, not in the mask.
- What's left is the covers' JPEG compression and resampling.
